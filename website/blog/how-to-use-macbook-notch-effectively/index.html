How to Use Your MacBook Notch More Effectively

By Aditya Gupta • Published on November 5, 2026 • Hardware • 5 min read

When Apple introduced the display notch on the 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro in late 2021, many users viewed it solely as a compromise—a necessary cutout to achieve thinner display borders around a 1080p FaceTime camera. Over time, however, thoughtful macOS developers have transformed the notch into an active, functional productivity asset.

Understanding the Physical Notch Architecture

The MacBook notch occupies approximately 210 physical pixels across and extends down through the top menu bar safe-area insets. Crucially, macOS does not treat this space as lost screen real estate:

  • The menu bar moves upward into the notch zone, reclaiming previously lost vertical pixel height for your main application windows below.
  • Apple's display controller enforces strict safe areas so standard application menu titles stay to the left, while system status icons stay to the right.

1. Using the Notch as an Eye-Level Anchor Point

Because your Mac's camera sensor is situated inside the notch housing, any element docked directly underneath it shares your camera's optical line of sight.

This geometric property makes the notch ideal for presentation tools. NotchFlow anchors teleprompter text directly beneath the notch cutout. When you read your script, your eyes naturally align with the camera lens, creating the illusion of direct eye contact with remote meeting participants without awkward gaze deviations.

2. Managing Menu Bar Overflow

One genuine challenge posed by the notch is menu bar overcrowding. If you run many background utility apps, icons on the right side of the menu bar can hide behind the notch cutout.

To solve this:

  • Use menu bar management tools (such as Hidden Bar or Ice) to collapse secondary icons behind an expandable disclosure arrow.
  • Remove redundant system indicators from System Settings > Control Center (e.g. Wi-Fi or Bluetooth if you rarely change networks).

3. Glanceable Status Interfaces

The notch area provides a natural peripheral zone for low-profile status updates that don't steal keyboard focus. Applications designed for the notch can display subtle indicators—such as reading progress, microphone activity, or speech tracking state—without covering active documents or terminal windows.

Summary

The MacBook notch is no longer just a housing for camera optics. By leveraging notch-aware utilities like NotchFlow, you can turn hardware constraints into an ergonomic advantage for daily presentations, remote meetings, and focused work.
