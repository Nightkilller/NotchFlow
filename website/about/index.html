About NotchFlow

The open source project bringing voice-synchronized teleprompting natively to macOS.

Why NotchFlow Exists

In modern remote communication—whether presenting quarterly business reviews, teaching online courses, pitching investors, or recording product demos—eye contact conveys credibility, confidence, and warmth. However, traditional presentation setups force a difficult compromise: you either memorize a script, look down at notes on your desk, or read from a window far away from your Mac's camera lens.

Commercial teleprompter rigs are bulky, expensive, and require dedicated hardware accessories. Software alternatives are often bloated Electron applications with monthly subscriptions, cloud dependencies, and noticeable speech lag.

NotchFlow was created by Aditya Gupta as an open-source, native macOS application designed from the ground up for Apple Silicon MacBooks. By positioning a lightweight reading pod directly under the physical display camera notch and running voice transcription locally via Apple Neural Engine, NotchFlow makes natural eye contact effortless and accessible to everyone.

Core Development Philosophy

  • Native Architecture: Built purely with Swift 6 and SwiftUI. NotchFlow boots instantly, consumes minimal memory, and integrates seamlessly with macOS desktop spaces and window management.
  • Zero Cloud Dependency: Your words and scripts belong to you. Transcription executes strictly on-device using Apple SpeechKit APIs. NotchFlow operates completely offline without network permissions.
  • Free & Open Source: Released under the permissive MIT License. The code is transparent, auditable, and community-driven on GitHub.
  • Minimalist Ergonomics: The interface stays out of your way. With idle auto-shrink and edge resizing, NotchFlow provides an unobtrusive reading guide that expands only when you need it.
